Keep the camera level across the bars. Tipping it forward along the bars, to reach under a feeder, is fine up to about 30°; tilting it across the bars makes corroded bars read nearly new. Take photo shows a level. Indoors, no flash; the bars can run up-and-down or across the frame. A dark pit behind the bars reads best, and sun or flash glare on wet bars gives bad readings. Where the floor under the bars is brighter than the bars (gravel, debris, glare on water) their edges can't be read, and the app leaves those readings out.

Tap the worst spot on the worst bar. The app measures the bar's width over one bar-spacing of length centred where you tap; the worst spot you tap is the result. Tap it again to clear it. Zoom in with two fingers to place it precisely.

Saved readings

Width loss = 1 − (bar width ÷ bar pitch) ÷ 0.4686, the width-to-pitch ratio of new tribar measured at C44, reported 1:1 as cross-section loss. The result is the worst spot you tap: the median width over one bar-spacing of length there (about ±9 points; the whole bar's median, shown alongside, is about ±5). The window percentages show the typical loss around it for comparison.
